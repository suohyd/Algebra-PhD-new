    Section 4.1 Semisimple algebras

    We assume that \(R\) is a commutative ring with unity and that \(A\) is a right \(R\)-algebra.
    🔗

    Definition 4.1.1. Semisimple algebra.

    An \(R\)-algebra \(A\) is said to be semisimple if \(A\) considered as a right \(A\)-module is semisimple module.
    🔗
    🔗

    Note 4.1.2.

    Suppose that \(A\) is a semisimple algebra. Then the simple right \(A\)-submodules are the minimal right ideals of \(A\text{.}\) Furthermore, as \(A\) is generated as a module over \(A\) by \(1_A\text{,}\) by Theorem 3.6.16, we have that
    \begin{equation*} A\simeq\bigoplus_{\substack{N_i\text{ minimal right ideals}\\1\leq i\leq n}}N_i. \end{equation*}
    🔗
    Using Theorem 3.8.5 we get the following. The algebra \(A\) is semisimple if and only if \(A_A\) is Artinian and \(\rad{A_A}=0\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    Checkpoint 4.1.3. Finite-dimensional algebra is simple if and only if radical is trivial.

    Suppose that \(F\) is a field and that \(A\) is a finite-dimensional algebra over \(F\text{.}\) Show that \(A\) is semisimple if and only if \(\rad{A_A}=0\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    Proposition 4.1.4.

    If \(A\) is a semisimple algebra, then every right \(A\)-module is semisimple. Furthermore, simple right \(A\)-modules are precisely the minimal right ideals of \(A\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    Proof.

    A free \(A\)-module is a direct sum of copies of \(A_A\text{.}\) By Corollary 3.4.12, every free \(A\)-module is semisimple. Suppose that \(M\) is a right \(A\)-module. Consider the direct sum of \(A_A\) with indexing set \(M\text{,}\) say \(F=\oplus_MA_A\text{.}\) For each \(m\in M\) define an \(A\)-module homomorphism \(f_m\colon A_A\to M\) such that \(f_m(1)=m\text{.}\) Using the universal property of the direct sum (see Proposition 3.4.5) there exists an \(A\)-module homomorphism \(f\colon\oplus_MA_A\to M\text{.}\) Note that \(f\) is surjective. By the previous observation, \(F\) is semisimple, so by Corollary 3.4.12, \(M\) is semisimple.
    🔗
    By Proposition 3.2.8, a simple right \(A\)-module is isomorphic to a quotient of \(A_A/\mathfrak{m}\) for some maximal right ideal of \(\mathfrak{m}\) of \(A\text{.}\) As \(A_A\) is semisimple, complement of \(\mathfrak{m}\) exists, say \(\mathfrak{n}\) be a right ideal of \(A\) such that \(A_A=\mathfrak{m}\oplus\mathfrak{n}\text{.}\) Hence, \(A_A/\mathfrak{m}\simeq\mathfrak{n}\text{.}\) By the correspondence theorem for submodules of a quotient modules, it follows that \(\mathfrak{n}\) must be a minimal right ideal of \(A\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    Corollary 4.1.5.

    Let \(A\) and \(B\) be \(R\)-algebras with \(A\) semisimple. If \(f\colon A\to B\) is a surjective \(R\)-algebra homomorphism, then \(B\) is semisimple.
    🔗
    🔗

    Proof.

    Using \(f\) we consider \(B\) as an \(A\)-module (see Definition 3.1.1). By the above Proposition 4.1.4, \(B\) is semisimple as an \(A\)-module. Suppose that \(N_i\) are simple \(A\)-modules such that \(B_A\simeq\oplus_iN_i\text{.}\) If \(a\in\ker f\text{,}\) then, for any \(b\in B\) we have \(b\cdot a=bf(a)=0\text{,}\) i.e., \(\ker f\subseteq\Ann{A}{B}\text{.}\) Furthermore, \(\Ann{A}{B}\subseteq\Ann{A}{N_i}\) for each \(i\text{,}\) i.e., \(\ker f\subseteq\Ann{A}{N_i}\text{.}\) Thus, by Proposition 3.1.8, for every \(i\text{,}\) there exists a simple right \(B\)-module \(M_i\) such that \(N_i\simeq {(M_i)}_A\text{.}\) Therefore, using Lemma 3.1.2, we get the following.
    \begin{equation*} B_A\simeq\oplus_iN_i\simeq\oplus_i(M_i)_A\simeq\left(\oplus_iM_i\right)_A \end{equation*}
    This shows that \(B\) is semisimple as a right \(B\)-module.
