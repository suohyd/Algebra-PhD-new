    Section 4.5 Matrices of homomorphisms

    We introduce a generalized matrix notation. Let \(R\) be a commutative ring with unity and \(A\) be a right \(R\)-algebra. Suppose that \(\left(M_1,M_2,\ldots,M_n\right)\) is a sequence of right \(A\)-modules. Note that \(\Hom{A}{M_j}{M_i}\) is a right \(A\)-module.
    🔗

    Definition 4.5.1. Matrices of homomorphism.

    Consider the following collection:
    \begin{equation*} \left[\Hom{A}{M_i}{M_i}\right]=\left\{\begin{bmatrix}\phi_{11}\amp\phi_{12}\amp\cdots\amp\phi_{1n}\\\phi_{21}\amp\phi_{22}\amp\cdots\amp\phi_{2n}\\\vdots\amp\vdots\amp\ddots\amp\vdots\\\phi_{1n}\amp\phi_{n2}\amp\cdots\amp\phi_{nn}\end{bmatrix}:\phi_{ij}\in\Hom{A}{M_j}{M_i}\right\} \end{equation*}
    For \([\phi_{ij}],[\psi_{ij}]\in\left[\Hom{A}{M_j}{M_i}\right]\) we define addition as
    \begin{equation*} [\phi_{ij}]+[\psi_{ij}]=[\phi_{ij}+\psi_{ij}], \end{equation*}
    where \(\phi_{ij}+\psi_{ij}\in\Hom{A}{M_j}{M_i}\) is obtained using \(A\)-module structure on \(\Hom{A}{M_j}{M_i}\text{.}\) Similarly, the scalar multiplication is obtained by using \(A\)-module structure on \(\Hom{A}{M_j}{M_i}\text{:}\)
    \begin{equation*} [\phi_{ij}]\cdot a=[\phi_{ij}\cdot a]. \end{equation*}
    Define the matrix multiplication as follows:
    \begin{equation*} [\phi_{ij}][\psi_{ij}]=\left[\sum_{k=1}^n\phi_{ik}\circ\psi_{kj}\right]. \end{equation*}
    Note that \(\phi_{ik}\circ\psi_{kj}\in\Hom{A}{M_j}{M_i}\) as
    \begin{equation*} M_j\xrightarrow{\psi_{kj}}M_k\xrightarrow{\phi_{ik}}M_i. \end{equation*}
    This makes \(\left[\Hom{A}{M_j}{M_i}\right]\) an \(R\)-algebra.
    🔗
    🔗

    Proposition 4.5.2.

    We have the following isomorphism of \(R\)-algebras.
    \begin{equation*} \left[\Hom{A}{M_j}{M_i}\right]\simeq\End{A}{M_1\oplus M_2\oplus\cdots\oplus M_n} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗

    Proof.

    Put \(M=M_1\oplus M_2\oplus\cdots\oplus M_n\text{.}\) Consider canonical projection homomorphisms \(\pi_i\colon M\to M_i\) and canonical inclusion homomorphisms \(\kappa_j\colon M_j\to M\text{,}\) i.e.,
    \begin{equation*} M_j\xrightarrow{\kappa_j} M\xrightarrow{\pi_i}M_i. \end{equation*}
    Hence we have the following.
    \begin{equation*} \sum_{j=1}^n\kappa_j\circ\pi_i=\unit_{M}\quad\text{and}\quad\pi_i\circ\kappa_j=0\text{ if }i\neq j\quad\text{and}\quad\pi_i\circ\kappa_i=\unit_{M_i} \end{equation*}
    We define \(R\)-algebra homomorphisms
    \begin{equation*} f\colon\left[\Hom{A}{M_j}{M_i}\right]\to\End{A}{M}\quad\text{by}\quad[\phi_{ij}]\mapsto\sum_{i,j}\kappa_i\circ\phi_{ij}\circ\pi_j \end{equation*}
    and
    \begin{equation*} g\colon\End{A}{M}\to\left[\Hom{A}{M_j}{M_i}\right]\quad\text{by}\quad \phi\mapsto\left[\pi_i\circ\phi\circ\kappa_j\right]. \end{equation*}
    Note that
    \begin{equation*} M\xrightarrow{\pi_j}M_j\xrightarrow{\phi_{ij}}M_i\xrightarrow{\kappa_i}M \end{equation*}
    and
    \begin{equation*} M_j\xrightarrow{\kappa_j}M\xrightarrow{\phi}M\xrightarrow{\pi_i}M_i. \end{equation*}
    We leave it to the reader to check that \(f,g\) are indeed \(R\)-algebra homomorphisms and they are mutual inverses.
    🔗
    🔗
    As a direct consequence of the above Proposition 4.5.2 we obtain the following corollary.
    🔗

    Corollary 4.5.3.

    If \(M\) is a right \(A\)-module then,
    \begin{equation*} \End{A}{\oplus_n\,M}\simeq M_n\left(\End{A}{M}\right). \end{equation*}
    As a particular case
    \begin{equation*} \End{A}{\oplus_n\,A_A}\simeq M_n(A). \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗

    Corollary 4.5.4.

    Let \(M_1,M_2,\cdots,M_n\) be right \(A\)-modules such that for \(i\neq j\text{,}\) \(\Hom{A}{M_j}{M_i}=0\text{.}\) Then,
    \begin{align*} \End{A}{M_1\oplus M_2\oplus\cdots\oplus M_n}\amp\simeq\End{A}{M_1}\oplus\End{A}{M_2}\oplus\cdots\oplus\End{A}{M_n} \\ \amp \simeq\End{A}{M_1}\times\End{A}{M_2}\times\cdots\times\End{A}{M_n} \end{align*}
    🔗
    🔗

    Proof.

    This follows from the universal property of direct sum, see Proposition 3.4.5 and from the fact that finite direct sum and direct product are equivalent.
    🔗
    🔗

    Checkpoint 4.5.5.

    Let \(A_1,A_2,\ldots,A_n\) be \(R\)-algebras (resp., \(R\)-modules). Show that direct sum of \(A_i\) and direct product of \(A_i\) are isomorphic as \(R\)-algebras (resp., as an \(R\)-modules).
    🔗
    Recall that if \(A_i\) with \(i\in I\) is a nonempty collection of \(R\)-algebras (resp., \(R\)-modules) with \(I\) not necessarily finite then, direct product of \(A_i\)
    \begin{equation*} \prod A_i=\left\{(x_i)_{i\in I}:x_i\in A_i\right\} \end{equation*}
    is an \(R\)-algebra (resp., \(R\)-modules) with componentwise addition and multiplication. It has the following universal property.
    🔗
    If \(B\) is an \(R\)-algebra (resp., \(R\)-module) with \(f_i\colon B\to A_i\) homomorphisms of \(R\)-algebras (resp., \(R\)-modules) for every \(i\text{.}\) Then, there exists a unique homomorphism \(\phi\colon B\to A\) such that \(f_i=\pi_i\circ\phi\text{,}\) where \(\pi_i\colon A\to A_i\) are canonical projections.
