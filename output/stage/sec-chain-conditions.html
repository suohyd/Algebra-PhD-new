    Section 3.6 Chain conditions

    We introduce in this section important classes of modules, viz., Artinian and Noetherian modules. In this section we assume that \(R\) is a commutative ring with unity and \(A\) is a right \(R\)-algebra. For an \(A\)-module \(M\) we denote by \(S(M)\) the collection of all \(A\)-submodules of \(M\text{.}\)
    🔗

    Definition 3.6.1. Artinian Module.

    An \(A\)-module \(M\) is said to be Artinian if there are no infinite, strictly decreasing sequences of submodules of \(M\text{,}\) i.e., if
    \begin{equation*} N_1\supsetneq N_2\supsetneq N_3\supsetneq\cdots \end{equation*}
    is a decreasing chain of \(A\)-submodules of \(M\) then, there exists \(\ell\in\N\) such that \(N_i=N_j\) for all \(i,j\geq\ell\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    Definition 3.6.2. Noetherian Module.

    An \(A\)-module \(M\) is said to be Noetherian if there are no infinite, strictly increasing sequences of submodules of \(M\text{,}\) i.e., if
    \begin{equation*} N_1\subsetneq N_2\subsetneq N_3\subsetneq\cdots \end{equation*}
    is a increasing chain of \(A\)-submodules of \(M\) then, there exists \(\ell\in\N\) such that \(N_i=N_j\) for all \(i,j\geq\ell\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    We often use the following equivalent conditions for Artinian/Noetherian modules.
    🔗

    Lemma 3.6.3.

    Following are equivalent for an \(A\)-module \(M\text{.}\)
    1. The module \(M\) is Noetherian (resp. Artinian);
      🔗
      🔗
    2. Every nonempty subset of \(S(M)\) has a maximal (resp., minimal) element.
      🔗
      🔗
    Furthermore, \(M\) is Noetherian if and only if every \(A\)-submodule of \(M\) is finitely generated.
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 3.6.4. Noetherian but not Artinian.

    If we consider \(\Z\) as a \(\Z\)-module then, \(\Z\) is Noetherian but not Artinian.
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 3.6.5. Artinian but not Noetherian.

    If we consider \(\Z(p^\infty)=\{a/p^n:a\in\Z,n\in\N\cup\{0\}\}\big/\Z\) as a \(\Z\)-module then, it is Artinian but not Noetherian.
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 3.6.6. Module which is both Artinian and Noetherian.

    Let \(V\) be a finite-dimensional vector space over a field \(F\text{.}\) Due to dimension reasons, \(V\) is both Artinian and Noetherian \(F\)-module.
    🔗
    Every finite abelian group as a \(\Z\)-module is both Artinian and Noetherian.
    🔗
    🔗

    Lemma 3.6.7.

    Let \(P,Q,R\) be \(A\)-submodules of \(M\) with \(P\subseteq Q\text{.}\) Then there exists the following exact sequence of \(A\)-modules.
    \begin{equation*} 0\to Q\cap R\big/P\cap R\to Q/P\to Q+R\big/P+R\to 0 \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗

    Lemma 3.6.8.

    Let \(0\to N\to M\to P\) be a short exact sequence of \(A\)-modules. The module \(M\) is Noetherian (resp. Artinian) if and only if \(N\) and \(P\) are Noetherian (resp. Artinian).
    🔗
    🔗

    Proposition 3.6.9.

    Suppose that an \(A\)-module \(M\) is both Artinian and Noetherian and that \(M\neq 0\text{.}\) Then, there exists a sequence of submodules of \(A\)
    \begin{equation*} 0=M_0\subsetneq M_1\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq M_{n-1}\subsetneq M_n=M \end{equation*}
    such that \(M_{i+1}/M_{i}\) are simple \(A\)-modules for \(i<n\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    Proof.

    By the assumption we have \(0=M_0\subsetneq M\text{.}\) Consider the set \(S_1\) of all the nonzero submodules of \(M\text{.}\) As \(M\in S_1\text{,}\) the set \(S_1\neq\emptyset\text{.}\) As \(M\) is Artinian, using Lemma 3.6.3, there exists a minimal element, say \(M_1\in S_1\text{.}\) Note that by the minimality \(M_1/M_0\simeq M_1\) is simple. We continue as before by considering the set of all submodules of \(M\) containing \(M_1\text{,}\) so as to obtain a submodule \(M_2\) with \(M_2/M_1\) simple. Continuing in this way one obtains a increasing chain of submodules
    \begin{equation*} 0=M_0\subsetneq M_1\subsetneq M_2\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq \end{equation*}
    such that \(M_{i+1}/M_i\) is simple. Now Noetherian hypothesis implies that there exists \(\ell\in\N\) such that \(M_\ell=M_i\) for \(i\geq\ell\text{.}\) It follows that \(M_\ell=M\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    Definition 3.6.10. Composition series and composition factors.

    A chain of \(A\)-submodules of \(M\text{,}\) \(0=M_0\subsetneq M_1\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq M_n=M\) is called a composition series of \(M\) if \(M_{i+1}/M_i\) is simple for \(i<n\text{.}\) The factor modules \(M_{i+1}/M_i\) are called composition factors of the series.
    🔗
    🔗

    Theorem 3.6.11.

    A nonzero \(A\)-module \(M\) is both Artinian and Noetherian if and only if \(M\) has a composition series.
    🔗
    🔗

    Proof.

    We have proved in Proposition 3.6.9 that if \(M\) is both Artinian and Noetherian then, \(M\) has a composition series.
    🔗
    🔗

    Theorem 3.6.12. Jordan-Hölder Theorem.

    Assume that \(M\) is an \(A\)-module. If \(0=M_0\subsetneq M_1\subsetneq M_2\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq M_n=M\) and \(0=M'_0\subsetneq M'_1\subsetneq M'_2\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq M'_k=M\) are composition series of \(M\) then, \(n=k\) and there is a permutation \(\sigma\in S_n\) such that \(M'_{j+1}/M'_j\simeq M_{\sigma(j+1)}/M_{\sigma(j)}\) for all \(j<n\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    Proof.

    Suppose that \(n=1\text{,}\) i.e., we have \(0=M_0\subsetneq M_1=M\) and that \(M_1/M_0\simeq M_1\) is simple. This shows that \(M\) can not have a nonzero proper submodule. Hence, \(k=1\text{.}\) Now we assume that \(n\geq 2\text{.}\) Consider the following chain of submodules of \(M\text{.}\)
    \begin{align*} 0=M_0'\cap M_{n-1}\subseteq M_1'\cap M_{n-1}\subseteq\amp\cdots\subseteq M_k'\cap M_{n-1}=M_{n-1}\\ \subseteq M_{n-1}=M'_0+M_{n-1}\subseteq M_1'+M_{n-1}\amp\subseteq\cdots\subseteq M_k'+M_{n-1}=M_n=M \end{align*}
    For \(j<k\) we have the following exact sequence of \(A\)-modules (see Lemma 3.6.7).
    \begin{equation*} 0\to \frac{M_{j+1}'\cap M_{n-1}}{M_j'\cap M_{n-1}}\to \frac{M_{j+1}'}{M_j'}\to \frac{M_{j+1}'+M_{n-1}}{M_j'+M_{n-1}}\to 0 \end{equation*}
    As \(\tfrac{M_{j+1}'}{M_j'}\) is simple either
    \begin{equation*} 0=\frac{M_{j+1}'\cap M_{n-1}}{M_j'\cap M_{n-1}}\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{M_{j+1}'}{{M_j'}}\simeq \frac{M_{j+1}'+M_{n-1}}{M_j'+M_{n-1}} \end{equation*}
    or
    \begin{equation*} 0=\frac{M_{j+1}'+M_{n-1}}{M_j'+M_{n-1}}\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{M_{j+1}'}{M_j'}\simeq \frac{M_{j+1}'\cap M_{n-1}}{M_j'\cap M_{n-1}}. \end{equation*}
    Furthermore, \(M_n/M_{n-1}\) is simple implies that there exists exactly one \(i<k\) such that \(M_{n-1}=M_i'+M_{n-1}\) and \(M_n=M_{i+1}'+M_{n-1}\text{.}\) Therefore, \(M_n/M_{n-1}\simeq M_{i+1}'/M_i'\) and
    \begin{equation*} 0=M_0'\cap M_{n-1}\subsetneq M_1'\cap M_{n-1}\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq M_{i-1}'\cap M_{n-1}\subsetneq M_i'\cap M_{n-1}{\color{blue}=}M_{i+1}'\cap M_{n-1}'\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq M_k'\cap M_{n-1} \end{equation*}
    is a composition series for \(M_{n-1}\text{.}\) By the induction hypothesis, \(k-1=n-1\) and there is a permutation \(\sigma'\in S_{n-1}\) such that \(M_{j+1}'/M_j'\simeq M_{\sigma'(j+1)}/M_{\sigma'(j)}\) for all \(j\neq i\text{.}\) We define \(\sigma\in S_n\) as \(\sigma(j)=\sigma'(j)\) for \(j\neq i\) and \(\sigma(i)=n-1\text{.}\) This gives a required result.
    🔗
    🔗

    Definition 3.6.13.

    We keep notations of Definition 3.6.10. By Theorem 3.6.12, the number \(n\) is unique for any composition series of \(M\text{.}\) This number is called the composition length of \(M\) and it is denoted by \(\ell(M)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    Corollary 3.6.14.

    Suppose that \(M, N\) and \(P\) are both Artinian and Noetherian \(A\)-modules. If \(0\to N\to M\to P\to 0\) is a short exact sequence of modules then,
    \begin{equation*} \ell(N)+\ell(P)=\ell(M). \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    We denote by \(\omega\) the first infinite ordinal number.
    🔗

    Theorem 3.6.15.

    Following conditions are equivalent for a nonzero semisimple \(A\)-module \(M\text{.}\)
    1. The module \(M\) is finitely generated as an \(A\)-module
      🔗
      🔗
    2. The module \(M\) is direct sum of finitely many simple modules.
      🔗
      🔗
    3. The module \(M\) is Artinian.
      🔗
      🔗
    4. The module \(M\) is Noetherian.
      🔗
      🔗
    5. There exists \(m<\omega\) such that if \(M_0\subsetneq M_1\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq M_k\) is finite then, \(k\leq m\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Proof.

    We first show that (1) and (2) are equivalent. Suppose that \(M\) is a finitely generated semisimpe module. Let \(M=\oplus_{i\in I}N_i\) with \(I\) infinite. If \(\{u_1,\ldots,u_r\}\) are finite generators of \(M\) then, there exists \(i_j\) such that \(u_j\in N_{i_j}\text{.}\) For any \(k>\max\{i_1,\ldots,i_r\}\text{,}\) an element \(u_k\in N_k\) is not \(A\)-linear combination of \(\{u_{i_j}\}\) because \(M\) is a direct sum of \(N_i\)’s. Therefore, \(I\) can not be infinite. Conversely, assume that \(M\) is a direct sum of finitely many simple \(A\)-modules, say \(M=\oplus_{i=1}^mN_i\text{.}\) By Proposition 3.2.8, there exists \(u_i\in N_i\) such that \(u_iA=N_i\) for each \(i\in\{1,2,\ldots,m\}\text{.}\) As \(M=\oplus_{i=1}^mN_i=\oplus_{i=1}^mu_iA\text{,}\) elements \(u_1,u_2,\ldots,u_m\) are finite number of generators of \(M\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Now we show that (2) and (3) (resp., (2) and (4)) are equivalent. Assume that \(M\) is a direct sum of finitely many simple modules. Every simple module is both Artinian and Noetherian. Therefore, by applying Lemma 3.6.8 finitely many times, we get that \(M\) is Artinian and Noetherian.
    🔗
    Assume condition (5). This implies that \(M\) is Noetherian. Hence, also Artinian due to equivalence of (1) to (4). Now assume that \(M\) is Artinian, hence also Noetherian. By Corollary 3.6.14, strictly increasing sequence of submodules of \(M\) can have at most \(\ell(M)+1\) terms.
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 3.6.16. Finitely generated non-Artinian (non-Noetherian) module.

    The hypothesis that \(M\) is semisimple in Theorem 3.6.15 is necessary. For instance, we may consider \(M=\Z\) as a \(\Z\)-module. The module is finitely generated, say by \(1\in\Z\text{.}\) However, \(M\) is not Artinian.
    🔗
    For non-Noetherian module we may consider the polynomial ring in infinitely many variables, say \(\Q[x_1,x_2,\ldots]\) as a module over itself. Then \(1\) generates the module and the module is not Noetherian.
