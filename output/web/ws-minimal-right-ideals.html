    Worksheet 4.4 Exercises related to minimal right ideals
    

    Most of the exercises are taken from [3]. We assume that \(R\) is a commutative ring with unity, and \(A\) is an \(R\)-algebra.
    🔗

    1.

    Let \(A\) be a semisimple algebra such that \(A=A_1\times A_2\times\cdots\times A_r\) with \(A_i\) simple algebras. Show that if \(N_i\) (resp., \(N_j\)) is a minimal right ideal of \(A_i\) (resp., \(A_j\)) for \(i\neq j\) then, \(\Hom{A}{N_j}{N_i}=0\text{.}\) In particular, \(N_i\not\simeq N_j\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    2.

    An element \(e\) of an \(R\)-algebra \(A\) is said to be idempotent if \(e^2=e\text{.}\) Prove the following statements.
    1. The algebra \(A\) is semisimple if and only if every right ideal of \(A\) is of the form \(eA\) for some idempotent \(e\in A\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    2. If \(A\) is semisimple and \(M\leq A_A\) then, \(M^2=M\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    3. If \(A\) is semisimple and \(I\) is a two-sided ideal of \(A\) then, there is a unique central idempotent \(e\in Z(A)\) such that \(I=eA\text{.}\) Conversely, show that if \(e\in Z(A)\) is an idempotent then, \(eA\) is a two-sided ideal of \(A\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    4. Let \(e_1,e_2,\ldots,e_n\in Z(A)\) be central idempotents such that \(1=e_1+e_2+\cdots+e_n\) and \(e_ie_j=0\) for \(i\neq j\text{.}\) Then, show that \(A=e_1A\times e_2A\times\cdots\times e_nA\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
