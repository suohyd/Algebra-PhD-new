    Section 4.4 Simple algebras

    We introduce ‘simple algebras’ in this section. Although the term ‘semisimple algebra’, introduced earlier, suggests that it is a generalization of simple algebra, it is not true without some additional conditions.
    🔗
    We assume that \(R\) is a commutative ring with unity and \(A\) is an \(R\)-algebra.
    🔗

    Definition 4.4.1. Simple algebra.

    An \(R\)-algebra \(A\) is said to be simple if its only two-sided ideals are \(\{0\}\) and \(A\) itself.
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 4.4.2. Quaternion algebra is simple over a field of characteristic not \(2\).

    We have shown in Proposition 2.1.7 that the quaternion algebra \((a,b)_F\) over a field of characteristic not equal to \(2\) is a simple algebra.
    🔗
    🔗
    In the next propostion we characterize simple algebras that are semisimple.
    🔗

    Proposition 4.4.3.

    For a simple algebra \(A\text{,}\) following are equivalent:
    1. \(A\) is semisimple;
      🔗
      🔗
    2. \(A_A\) is right Artinian;
      🔗
      🔗
    3. \(A\) has a minimal right ideal.
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Proof.

    Suppose that \(A\) is a simple algebra as well as a semisimple algebra. We show that \(A_A\) is right Artinian. Note that for any nonzero \(a\in A\text{,}\) the two-sided ideal \(AaA\) is nonzero and hence, \(AaA=A\text{.}\) Therefore, there are \(x_i,y_i\in A\) such that \(1=\sum_{i=1}^n x_iay_i\text{.}\) This implies that \(A_A\) is generated by \(\{x_1a,x_2a,\ldots,x_na\}\text{,}\) i.e., \(A_A\) is finitely generated. In view of Theorem 3.6.16, \(A_A\) is right Artinian. This shows that (1) implies (2).
    🔗
    If \(A_A\) is right Artinian then, by Lemma 3.6.3, \(A_A\) has a minimal right ideal. This shows that (2) implies (3).
    🔗
    Now assume that \(N\) is a minimal right ideal of \(A\text{.}\) Hence, \(N\neq 0\) and \(AN\text{,}\) a two-sided ideal of \(A\) generated by \(N\text{,}\) is \(A\text{.}\) Thus,
    \begin{equation*} A=\sum_{a\in A}aN. \end{equation*}
    Note that each \(aN\) is a right ideal of \(A\text{,}\) i.e., each \(aN\) is an \(A\)-module. Furthermore, each \(aN\simeq N\) as an \(A\)-modules. Hence, \(A\) is written as a sum of simple \(A\)-modules, i.e., \(A\) is a semisimple algebra. This proves that (3) implies (1).
    🔗
    🔗

    Proposition 4.4.4.

    Following statements are equivalent for a semisimple algebra \(A\text{:}\)
    1. \(A\) is simple;
      🔗
      🔗
    2. any two minimal right ideals of \(A\) are isomorphic;
      🔗
      🔗
    3. any two simple \(A\)-modules are isomorphic.
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Proof.

    Suppose that \(A\) is simple. Let \(M\) and \(N\) are minimal right ideals of \(A\text{.}\) Thus, \(AM\) and \(AN\) are nonzero ideals of \(A\text{.}\) As \(A\) is simple, we have \(AM=A=AN\text{.}\) Therefore,
    \begin{equation*} A(MN)=(AM)N=AN=A. \end{equation*}
    In particular, \(MN\neq 0\text{.}\) By Proposition 4.3.5, \(M\simeq N\text{.}\) This shows that (1) implies (2).
    🔗
    In view of Proposition 4.1.4, (2) and (3) are equivalent.
    🔗
