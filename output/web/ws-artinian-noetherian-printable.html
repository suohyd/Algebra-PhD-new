    Worksheet 3.7 Exercises related to Artinian and Noetherian modules
    

    Most of the exercises are taken from [3] and [1]. Throughout this worsheet we assume that \(R\) is a commutative ring with unity and that \(A\) is a right \(R\)-algebra. We assume that \(M\) is a right \(A\)-module.
    🔗

    1.

    Show that if \(A_A\) is Noetherian (resp., Artinian) then, every finitely-generated right \(A\)-module is Noetherian (resp., Artinian). In particular, show that if \(A\) is a division ring and \(M\) is a finitely-generated right \(A\)-module, then \(M\) is Noetherian and Artinian.
    🔗
    🔗

    2.

    Let \(f\colon M\to M\) be an \(A\)-module endomorphism.
    1. Show that if \(M\) is Artinian then, \(\Image{f^n}+\ker f^n=M\) for some positive integer \(n\text{.}\) Use this to show that \(f\) is an isomorphism if and only if it is a monomorphism.
      🔗
      🔗
    2. Show that if \(M\) is Noetherian then, \(\Image{f^n}\cap\ker f^n=0\) for some positive integer \(n\text{.}\) Use this to show that \(f\) is an isomorphism if and only if it is an epimorphism.
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    3. Fitting’s lemma.

    If \(M\) is a module with a composition series, and \(f\colon M\to M\) is an endomorphism, show that there exists a positive integer \(n\) such that
    \begin{equation*} M=\Image{f^n}\oplus\ker f^n. \end{equation*}
    Furthermore, show that the restriction of \(f\) to \(\Image{f^n}\) is an automorphism and the restriction of \(f\) to \(\ker f^n\) is a nilpotent endomorphism.
    🔗
    🔗

    4.

    Let \(M\) be a right Artinian (resp., Noetherian) \(A\)-module. Show that if \(M^{(r)}\simeq M^{(s)}\) for some positive integers \(r,s\text{,}\) then \(r=s\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Recall that \(M^{(n)}\) denotes the direct sum of \(n\)-copies of \(M\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Hint.
    We may assume that \(r<s\) and that \(M\) is Noetherian. Consider the projection homomorphism \(\pi\colon M^{(s)}\to M^{(r)}\text{,}\) and suppose that \(\varphi\colon M^{(s)}\xrightarrow{\sim}M^{(r)}\) is the given isomorphism. Consider the endomorphism \(f=\varphi\circ\pi\text{.}\) As \(\varphi\) and \(\pi\) are surjective, so is \(f\text{.}\) Using Worksheet Exercise 3.7.2, we conclude that \(f\) is an isomorphism. In particular, \(\pi\) is an isomorphism onto its image, which is impossible if \(r<s\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    5.

    Let \(V\) be a vector space over a field \(F\) with a countable basis \(\{v_1,v_2,\ldots\}\text{.}\) Suppose that \(T\) is an \(F\)-linear endomorphism of \(V\text{.}\) Using \(T\) we consider \(V\) as a right \(F[t]\)-module.
    1. Let \(T\colon V\to V\) be defined by \(T(v_1)=0\) and \(T(v_{i+1})=v_i\) for \(i=1,2,3,\ldots\text{.}\) Show that \(F[t]\)-module \(V\) is Artinian.
      🔗
      🔗
    2. Let \(T\colon V\to V\) be defined by \(T(v_i)=v_{i+1}\) for \(i=1,2,3,\ldots\text{.}\) Show that \(F[t]\)-module \(V\) is Noetherian.
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    6.

    Let \(M\) be a right \(A\)-module of finite length \(\ell(M)\text{,}\) and \(L,N\) be submodules of \(M\text{.}\) Show the following.
    \begin{equation*} \ell(L+N)+\ell(L\cap N)=\ell(L)+\ell(N) \end{equation*}
    🔗
    Hint.
    Use the isomorphism \((L+N)\big/N\simeq L\big/(L\cap N)\) and the following short exact sequences.
    \begin{equation*} 0\to N\to L+N\to (L+N)/N\to 0 \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} 0\to L\cap N\to L\to L/(L\cap N) \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    7.

    Let \(N_1,N_2,\ldots,N_k\) be submodules of \(M\) with each \(M/N_i\) has finite length. Show that \(M/\bigcap_{i=1}^k N_i\) has finite length.
    🔗
    🔗

    8.

    1. Determine the length of the \(\Z\)-module \(\Z/{n}\Z\) for a positive integer \(n\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    2. Determine for which positive integers \(n\text{,}\) the module \(\Z/n\Z\) has a unique composition series.
      🔗
      🔗
    3. Find all compostion series of \(\Z/12\Z\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗
    Hint.
    1. Suppose that \(n=p^k\) for some prime number \(p\) and some positive integer \(k\text{.}\) Show that the length of the \(\Z\)-module \(\Z_{p^k}\) is \(k\text{.}\) For instance,
      \begin{equation*} 0=p^k\Z/p^k\Z\subsetneq p^{k-1}\Z/p^k\Z\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq p\Z/p^k\Z\subsetneq\Z/p^k\Z \end{equation*}
      is a composition series of \(\Z_{p^k}\text{.}\)
      🔗
      For a general positive integer \(n\text{,}\) use the Chinese Remainder Theorem to write \(\Z_n\) as a direct sum of \(\Z_{p_i^{k_i}}\)’s and use the previous part along with Corollary 3.6.15 to conclude.
      🔗
      🔗
