    Section 3.5 Chain conditions

    We introduce in this section important classes of modules, viz., Artinian and Noetherian modules. In this section we assume that \(R\) is a commutative ring with unity and \(A\) is a right \(R\)-algebra. For an \(A\)-module \(M\) we denote by \(S(M)\) the collection of all \(A\)-submodules of \(M\text{.}\)
    🔗

    Definition 3.5.1. Artinian Module.

    An \(A\)-module \(M\) is said to be Artinian if there are no infinite, strictly decreasing sequences of submodules of \(M\text{,}\) i.e., if
    \begin{equation*} N_1\supsetneq N_2\supsetneq N_3\supsetneq\cdots \end{equation*}
    is a decreasing chain of \(A\)-submodules of \(M\) then, there exists \(\ell\in\N\) such that \(N_i=N_j\) for all \(i,j\geq\ell\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    Definition 3.5.2. Noetherian Module.

    An \(A\)-module \(M\) is said to be Noetherian if there are no infinite, strictly increasing sequences of submodules of \(M\text{,}\) i.e., if
    \begin{equation*} N_1\subsetneq N_2\subsetneq N_3\subsetneq\cdots \end{equation*}
    is a increasing chain of \(A\)-submodules of \(M\) then, there exists \(\ell\in\N\) such that \(N_i=N_j\) for all \(i,j\geq\ell\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    We often use the following equivalent conditions for Artinian/Noetherian modules.
    🔗

    Lemma 3.5.3.

    Following are equivalent for an \(A\)-module \(M\text{.}\)
    1. The module \(M\) is Noetherian (resp. Artinian);
      🔗
      🔗
    2. Every nonempty subset of \(S(M)\) has a maximal (resp., minimal) element.
      🔗
      🔗
    Furthermore, \(M\) is Noetherian if and only if every \(A\)-submodule of \(M\) is finitely generated.
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 3.5.4. Noetherian but not Artinian.

    If we consider \(\Z\) as a \(\Z\)-module then, \(\Z\) is Noetherian but not Artinian.
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 3.5.5. Artinian but not Noetherian.

    If we consider \(\Z(p^\infty)=\{a/p^n:a\in\Z,n\in\N\}\) as a \(\Z\)-module then, \(\Z\) is Artinian but not Noetherian.
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 3.5.6. Module which is both Artinian and Noetherian.

    Let \(V\) be a finite-dimensional vector space over a field \(F\text{.}\) Due to dimension reasons, \(V\) is both Artinian and Noetherian \(F\)-module.
    🔗
    Every finite abelian group as a \(\Z\)-module is both Artinian and Noetherian.
    🔗
    🔗

    Lemma 3.5.7.

    Let \(P,Q,R\) be \(A\)-submodules of \(M\) with \(P\subseteq Q\text{.}\) Then there exists the following exact sequence of \(A\)-modules.
    \begin{equation*} 0\to Q\cap R\big/P\cap R\to Q/P\to Q+R\big/P+R\to 0 \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗

    Lemma 3.5.8.

    Let \(0\to N\to M\to P\) be a short exact sequence of \(A\)-modules. The module \(M\) is Noetherian (resp. Artinian) if and only if \(N\) and \(P\) are Noetherian (resp. Artinian).
    🔗
    🔗

    Proposition 3.5.9.

    Suppose that an \(A\)-module \(M\) is both Artinian and Noetherian and that \(M\neq 0\text{.}\) Then, there exists a sequence of submodules of \(A\)
    \begin{equation*} 0=M_0\subsetneq M_1\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq M_{n-1}\subsetneq M_n=M \end{equation*}
    such that \(M_{i+1}/M_{i}\) are simple \(A\)-modules for \(i<n\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    Proof.

    By the assumption we have \(0=M_0\subsetneq M\text{.}\) Consider the set \(S_1\) of all the nonzero submodules of \(M\text{.}\) As \(M\in S_1\text{,}\) the set \(S_1\neq\emptyset\text{.}\) As \(M\) is Artinian, using Lemma 3.5.3, there exists a minimal element, say \(M_1\in S_1\text{.}\) Note that by the minimality \(M_1/M_0\simeq M_1\) is simple. We continue as before by considering the set of all submodules of \(M\) containing \(M_1\text{,}\) so as to obtain a submodule \(M_2\) with \(M_2/M_1\) simple. Continuing in this way one obtains a increasing chain of submodules
    \begin{equation*} 0=M_0\subsetneq M_1\subsetneq M_2\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq \end{equation*}
    such that \(M_{i+1}/M_i\) is simple. Now Noetherian hypothesis implies that there exists \(\ell\in\N\) such that \(M_\ell=M_i\) for \(i\geq\ell\text{.}\) It follows that \(M_\ell=M\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    Definition 3.5.10. Composition series and composition factors.

    A chain of \(A\)-submodules of \(M\text{,}\) \(0=M_0\subsetneq M_1\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq M_n=M\) is called a composition series of \(M\) if \(M_{i+1}/M_i\) is simple for \(i<n\text{.}\) The factor modules \(M_{i+1}/M_i\) are called composition factors of the series.
    🔗
    🔗

    Theorem 3.5.11.

    If \(0=M_0\subsetneq M_1\subsetneq M_2\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq M_n=M\) and \(0=M_0\subsetneq M_1\subsetneq M_2\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq M'_k=M\) are composition series of \(M\) then, \(n=k\) and there is a permutation \(\sigma\in S_n\) such that \(M_{j+1}/M_j\simeq M_{\sigma(j+1)}/M_{\sigma(j)}\) for all \(j<n\text{.}\)
    🔗
