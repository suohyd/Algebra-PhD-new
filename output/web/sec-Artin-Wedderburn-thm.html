    Section 4.6 Artin-Wedderburn Theorem

    We are now in a position to state and proof Artin-Wedderburn theorem. As usual we assume that \(R\) is a commutative ring with unity and that \(A\) is an \(R\)-algebra.
    🔗

    Theorem 4.6.1. Artin-Wedderburn.

    Let \(A\) be a right or a left \(R\)-algebra.
    1. There exists natural numbers \(n_1,n_2,\ldots,n_r\) and \(R\)-division algebras \(D_1,D_2,\ldots,D_r\) such that
      \begin{equation} A\simeq M_{n_1}(D_1)\times M_{n_2}(D_2)\times\cdots\times M_{n_r}(D_r).\tag{4.6.1} \end{equation}
      🔗
      🔗
    2. The pairs \((n_1,D_1),(n_2,D_2),\ldots,(n_r,D_r)\) in (4.6.1) are unique, up to an isomorphism.
      🔗
      🔗
    3. Conversely, if \(n_1,n_2,\ldots,n_r\in\N\) and \(D_1,D_2,\ldots,D_r\) are division algebras over \(R\) then,
      \begin{equation*} M_{n_1}(D_1)\times M_{n_2}(D_2)\times\cdots\times M_{n_r}(D_r) \end{equation*}
      is a right and a left semisimple \(R\)-algebra.
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Proof.

    Suppose that \(A_A\) is semisimple. As \(A_A\) is generated by \(1_A\) we have that \(A_A\simeq\oplus_{i\in I}N_i\text{,}\) where \(N_i\) are simple \(A\)-modules, i.e., \(N_i\) are minimal right ideals of \(A\text{,}\) and \(|I|<\infty\) (see Theorem 3.6.16). Thus, \(A_A\simeq M_1\oplus M_2\oplus\cdots\oplus M_r\) with each \(M_i\) is a direct sum of \(n_i\) copies of a minimal right ideals \(N_i\text{,}\) and \(N_i\not\simeq N_j\) for \(i\neq j\text{.}\) We claim that \(\Hom{A}{M_j}{M_i}=0\) for \(i\neq j\text{.}\) Indeed, let \(f\in\Hom{A}{M_j}{M_i}\text{.}\) We have \(M_j=\oplus_{k=1}^{n_j}N_{jk}\) with \(N_{jk}\simeq N_j\) and \(M_i=\oplus_{\ell=1}^{n_i}N_{i\ell}\) with \(N_{i\ell}\simeq N_i\text{.}\) As \(N_{jk}\) is simple \(A\)-module, either \(f|_{N_{jk}}=0\) or \(f|_{N_{jk}}(N_{jk})\simeq N_j\subseteq M_i\text{.}\) Suppose that \(0\neq f|_{N_{jk}}(N_{jk})\simeq N_j\subseteq M_i\) By Lemma 3.4.6, there exists \(J\subset\{1,2,\ldots,n_i\}\) such that
    \begin{equation*} M_i=f(N_{jk})\oplus\left(\oplus_{\ell\in J}N_{i\ell}\right)\text{.} \end{equation*}
    This implies that
    \begin{equation*} M_i\big/\left(\oplus_{\ell\in J}N_{i\ell}\right)\simeq\bigoplus_{\ell\in\{1,2,\ldots,n_i\}\setminus J}N_{i\ell}\simeq f\left(N_{jk}\right). \end{equation*}
    Hence, \(|\{1,2,\ldots,n_i\}\setminus J|=1\) and thus, \(N_j\simeq f\left(N_{jk}\right)\simeq N_i\text{,}\) a contradiction. This shows that \(f|_{N_{jk}}=0\) for every \(j\text{,}\) and hence our claim is proved. Therefore,
    \begin{align*} A\amp\simeq\End{A}{A_A}\amp \\ \amp\simeq\End{A}{M_1\oplus M_2\oplus\cdots\oplus M_r}\amp\\ \amp\simeq \End{A}{M_1}\times\End{A}{M_2}\times\cdots\times\End{A}{M_r}\amp\text{by }\knowl{./knowl/xref/cor-endomorphism-algebra-as-diagonal-matrices.html}{\text{Corollary 4.5.4}}\\ \amp\simeq M_{n_1}\left(\End{A}{N_1}\right)\times M_{n_2}\left(\End{A}{N_2}\right)\times\cdots\times M_{n_r}\left(\End{A}{N_r}\right)\amp \text{by }\knowl{./knowl/xref/cor-endomorphism-as-matrices.html}{\text{Corollary 4.5.3}} \\ \amp\simeq M_{n_1}(D_1)\times M_{n_2}(D_2)\times\cdots\times M_{n_r}(D_r)\amp \text{by }\knowl{./knowl/xref/lem-schur-lemma.html}{\text{Lemma 3.2.9}},\, \End{A}{N_i}=D_i\text{ is division}. \end{align*}
