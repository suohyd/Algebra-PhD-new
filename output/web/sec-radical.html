    Section 3.8 Radical and Socle of a module

    We assume that \(R\) is a commutative ring with unity, and that \(A\) is a right \(R\)-algebra. Consider \(M\) as a right \(A\)-module.
    🔗

    Definition 3.8.1.

    The radical of a right \(A\)-module \(M\) is
    \begin{equation*} \rad{M}=\bigcap_{\substack{N\leq M\\M/N\text{ is simple}}}N \end{equation*}
    If \(M\) has no \(A\)-submodule \(N\) such that \(M/N\) is simple then, we write \(\rad{M}=M\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 3.8.2. Radical of \(\Q\) as a \(\Z\)-module is \(\Q\).

    Consider \(\Q\) as a \(\Z\)-module. Suppose there is a proper submodule \(N\) of \(\Q\) such that \(\Q/N\) is simple. By Proposition 3.2.8, there exists a prime number \(p\) such that \(\Q/N\simeq\Z/p\Z\) as \(\Z\)-modules. Thus, for any \(x\in\Q\text{,}\) \(px\in N\text{.}\) Choose \(x\in\Q\setminus N\text{.}\) Then there is \(y\in\Q\) such that \(py=x\text{.}\) Note that \(x=py\in N\text{,}\) a contradiction.
    🔗
    🔗
    We collect a few observations related to radical in the following lemma.
    🔗

    Lemma 3.8.3.

    Let \(M\) be a right \(A\)-module.
    1. The radical of \(M\) is an \(A\)-submodule of \(M\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    2. If \(N\leq M\) and \(\rad{(M/N)}=0\) then, \(\rad{M}\subseteq N\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    3. The radical of \(M/\rad{M}\) is zero.
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Lemma 3.8.4.

    If a right \(A\)-module \(M\) is semisimple then, \(\rad{M}=0\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    Proof.

    Let \(M=\oplus_{i\in I}N_i\text{,}\) where \(N_i\) is a simple \(A\)-module. For every \(i\in I\) consider \(L_i=\oplus_{k\neq i}N_k\text{.}\) Then, \(M/L_i\simeq N_i\) is simple for each \(i\in I\text{.}\) Thus, \(\rad{M}\subseteq\cap_iL_i=\{0\}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    Theorem 3.8.5.

    A right \(A\)-module \(M\) is finitely generated and semisimple if and only if \(M\) is Artinian and \(\rad{M}=0\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    Proof.

    Suppose that \(M\) is a finitely generated and semisimple \(A\)-module. By Theorem 3.6.16 and Lemma 3.8.4, the module \(M\) is Artinian and its radical is trivial.
    🔗
    Conversely, assume that \(M\) is Artinian and that \(\rad{M}=0\text{.}\) Suppose that \(\rad{M}\) is the inversection of \(N_i\) (\(i\in I\)) such that \(M/N_i\) is simple. Note that as \(\rad{M}=0\text{,}\) the set \(I\) is not empty. Consider the following collection of \(A\)-submodules of \(M\)
    \begin{equation*} \Sigma=\{N_{i_1}\cap\cdots\cap N_{i_k}:i_1,\ldots,i_k\in I\} \end{equation*}
    This is a nonempty collection of submodules of an Artinian module, hence, by Lemma 3.6.3, the collection \(\Sigma\) has a minimal element, say \(N_1\cap\cdots\cap N_r\text{.}\) We first observe that this minimal element is zero. Indeed, if \(N_1\cap\cdots\cap N_r\neq 0\) then for some \(i\in I\text{,}\) \(N_1\cap\cdots\cap N_r\) is not a subset of \(N_i\) and hence, \(N_1\cap\cdots\cap N_r\cap N_i\subsetneq N_1\cap\cdots\cap N_r\text{,}\) a contradiction to minimality. Therefore, \(N_1\cap\cdots\cap N_r=0\text{.}\) We define an \(A\)-module homomorphism \(\varphi\colon M\to\oplus_{i=1}^rM/N_i\) by
    \begin{equation*} m\mapsto(m+N_1,\ldots,m+N_r). \end{equation*}
    This is a surjective map with the kernel \(N_1\cap\cdots\cap N_r\text{.}\) Hence,
    \begin{equation*} M\simeq (M/N_1)\oplus\cdots\oplus(M/N_r). \end{equation*}
    This shows that \(M\) is semisimple. It is also finitely generated because \(M\) given to be Artinian (see Theorem 3.6.16).
    🔗
    🔗

    Definition 3.8.6. Socle of a module.

    Let \(M\) be a right \(A\)-module. The socle of \(M\text{,}\) denoted by \(\soc{M}\text{,}\) is defined as
    \begin{equation*} \soc{M}=\sum\left\{N\leq M:N\text{ is simple}\right\}. \end{equation*}
    If \(M\) has no simple \(A\)-submodule then, we write \(\soc{M}=0\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    Note 3.8.7. Socle is maximal semisimple submodule.

    By Corollary 3.4.7, it follows that \(\soc{M}\) is a semisimple \(A\)-module, and by the definition of socle, it can be considered as a maximal semisimple submodule. In particular, \(M\) is semisimple if and only if \(\soc{M}=M\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 3.8.8. Socle and radical of \(\R[x]/(x^n)\) as an \(\R[x]\)-module.

    Let \(M=\R[x]/(x^2)\) be an \(\R[x]\)-module. We calculate the socle and the radical of \(M\text{.}\) By the correspondence theorem, submodules of \(M\) are submodules of \(\R[x]\) containing \((x^2)\text{.}\) Note that the only such submodules are \((x^2)\text{,}\) \((x)\text{,}\) and \(\R[x]\text{.}\) Thus, the only proper nontrivial submodule of \(M\) is \((x)/(x^2)\text{.}\) As \(M/((x)/(x^2))\simeq\R\) is simple, we have \(\rad{M}=(x)/(x^2)\text{.}\) Also, \((x)/(x^2)\) is the only simple submodule of \(M\) and hence, \(\soc{M}=(x)/(x^2)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    More generally, for \(n\geq 1\text{,}\) let \(M_n=\R[x]/(x^n)\) be an \(\R[x]\)-module. Then, by similar arguments as above, we have \(\rad{M_n}=(x)/(x^n)\) and \(\soc{M_n}=(x^{n-1})/(x^n)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 3.8.9. Socle and radical of an integral domain which is not a field.

    Let \(A\) be an integral domain which is not a field. Consider \(A\) as a right \(A\)-module. So, the simple \(A\)-modules are nonzero minimal ideals of \(A\text{.}\) As every ideal is contained in a maximal and hence a prime ideal, we need to find nonzero minimal prime ideals of \(A\text{.}\) However, there are no nonzero minimal prime ideals in an integral domain which is not a field.

    Aside: Why there are no nonzero minimal prime ideals in an integral domain which is not a field?

    Let \(\mathfrak{p}\) be a nonzero minimal prime ideal of \(A\text{.}\) Consider \(x/1\in A_{\mathfrak{p}}\text{.}\) As \(\mathfrak{p}A_\mathfrak{p}\) is the unique prime ideal of \(A_{\mathfrak{p}}\text{,}\) we have \(x^n/1=0\in A_{\mathfrak{p}}\text{,}\) i.e., there exists \(s\in A\setminus\mathfrak{p}\) such that \(sx^n=0\text{.}\) As \(A\) is an integral domain, \(x=0\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Hence, \(\rad{M}=M\) and \(\soc{M}=0\text{.}\)
    🔗
    If \(A\) is a field then, as an \(A\)-module, \(\rad{A}=0\) and \(\soc{A}=A\text{.}\) In fact for any vector space \(V\) over \(A\text{,}\) \(\rad{V}=0\) and \(\soc{V}=V\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    Lemma 3.8.10.

    The socle of \(A_A\) is a two-sided ideal of \(A\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    Proof.

    As \(\soc{A_A}\) is an \(A\)-submodule of \(A_A\text{,}\) it is a right ideal. Now suppose that \(a\in A\text{.}\) We show that \(ax\in\soc{A_A}\) for any \(x\in\soc{A_A}\text{.}\) Suppose that \(x=n_1+n_2+\cdots+n_r\) with \(n_i\in N_i\) and \(N_i\) is a simple \(A\)-submodules of \(A_A\text{.}\) If \(ax=0\) then we are done. So, we assume that \(ax\neq 0\text{.}\) So there exists \(i\) such that \(an_i\neq 0\text{.}\) As \(N_i\) is simple, \(N_i=n_iA\text{.}\) Thus, there exists \(b\in A\) such that \(n_i=n_ib\text{.}\) Therefore, \(an_i=a(n_ib)\text{.}\) This shows that \(aN_i\) is a nonzero right ideal of \(A\text{.}\) Consider a map \(f_i\colon N_i=n_iA\to aN_i\) given by \(n_iy\mapsto an_iy\text{.}\) This is a surjective map. For any \(z\in A\) we have \(f(n_iz)=a(n_iz)=(an_i)z=f(n_i)z\) and that \(f(n_iy+n_iz)=f(n_i(y+z))=an_i(y+z)=f(n_iy)+f(n_iz)\text{.}\) Therefore, \(f_i\) is an \(A\)-module homomorphism. Furthermore, \(f(n_i)=an_i\neq 0\text{,}\) and hence \(f_i\) is injective because \(N_i\) is simple. Thus, \(aN_i\) is simple and hence \(aN_i\subseteq\soc{A_A}\text{.}\) As \(a\in A\) was arbitrarry, \(\soc{A_A}\) is a two-sided ideal of \(A\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    Definition 3.8.11. Essential submodule.

    An \(A\)-submodule \(N\) of a right \(A\)-module \(M\) is said to be essential if for every nonzero \(A\)-submodule \(L\) of \(M\text{,}\) \(N\cap L\neq 0\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    Note 3.8.12.

    An essential submodule is always nonzero.
    🔗
    🔗

    Lemma 3.8.13.

    The socle of a right \(A\)-module \(M\) is the intersection of all essential \(A\)-submodules of \(M\text{.}\)
    \begin{equation*} \soc{M}=\bigcap_{\substack{E\leq M\\E\text{ is essential}}}E \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗

    Proof.

    For a simple submodule \(N\leq M\) and a nonzero \(u\in N\text{,}\) we have \(N=uA\text{.}\) Thus, if \(E\leq M\) is an essential submodule then, \(N\cap E\neq 0\) and hence, \(N\subseteq E\text{.}\) This shows that \(\soc{M}\) is contained in every essential submodule of \(M\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Conversely, let \(S\) be the intersection of all essential \(A\)-submodules of \(M\text{.}\) We show that \(S\) is semisimple by showing that any submodule of \(S\) has a complement in \(S\text{.}\) Let \(N\leq S\text{.}\) Consider the following collection of submodules of \(M\text{.}\)
    \begin{equation*} \Sigma=\{N'\leq M:N'\cap N=0\} \end{equation*}
    As \(0\in\Sigma\text{,}\) the collection \(\Sigma\neq\emptyset\text{.}\) Suppose that \(\{N_i'\}\) is a chain in \(\Sigma\text{.}\) We show that \(\cup N_i'\in\Sigma\text{.}\) Indeed, if \(x\in(\cup N_i')\cap N\) then, \(x\in N_j'\) for some \(j\text{.}\) Thus, \(x=0\) as \(N_j'\in\Sigma\text{.}\) By Zorn’s lemma, there exists a maximal element \(N'\) in \(\Sigma\text{.}\) We claim that \(N+N'\) is an essential submodule of \(M\text{.}\) Suppose not, then there exists a nonzero submodule \(L\leq M\) such that \((N+N')\cap L=0\text{.}\) If there exists \(x\in N\cap (L+N')\) then, \(x=\ell+n'\) for \(\ell\in L\) and \(n'\in N'\text{.}\) Thus, \(\ell=x-n'\in L\cap (N+N')\text{.}\) This shows that \(N\cap(L+N')=0\) and hence, \(N'+L\in\Sigma\text{,}\) contradicting the maximality of \(N'\text{.}\) Thus, \(N+N'\) is an essential submodule of \(M\) and hence, it contains \(S\text{.}\) This shows that \(S=N+(N'\cap S)\text{.}\) As \(N+N'=N\oplus N'\) we have \(S=N\oplus(N'\cap S)\text{.}\) Therefore, every submodule of \(S\) has a complement in \(S\) and hence, \(S\) is semisimple (see Corollary 3.4.8). This shows that \(S\subseteq\soc{M}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    Lemma 3.8.14.

    If \(M\) is an Artinian right \(A\)-module then, \(\soc{M}\) is an essential \(A\)-submodule of \(M\text{.}\) In particular, \(\soc{M}\neq 0\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    Proof.

    Let \(N\leq M\) be a nonzero \(A\)-submodule. As \(M\) is Artinian, so is \(N\text{.}\) Therefore, \(N\) has a simple submodule, say \(S\) (see Corollary 3.6.4). By definition of socle, \(S\leq\soc{M}\) and hence, \(N\cap\soc{M}\neq 0\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    Corollary 3.8.15.

    Let \(M\) be a nonzero Artinian right \(A\)-module. If \(f\colon M\to N\) is an \(A\)-module homomorphism such that the restriction \(f|_{\soc{M}}\colon\soc{M}\to N\) is injective then, \(f\) is injective.
    🔗
    🔗

    Proof.

    By Lemma 3.8.14, \(\soc{M}\neq 0\) is an essential submodule of \(M\text{.}\) So, if \(\ker f\neq 0\) then, \(\soc{M}\cap\ker f\neq 0\text{,}\) a contradiction to the injectivity of \(f|_{\soc{M}}\text{.}\)
